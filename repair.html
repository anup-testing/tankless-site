Common Tankless Water Heater Problems and What Causes Them

No Hot Water

A complete loss of hot water usually stems from power or gas supply interruptions, ignition failure, or flow sensor issues. Venting restrictions or safety shutdowns can also stop the heating process before it starts.

  • Power or gas interruption
  • Ignition failure
  • Flow sensor fault
  • Venting restriction
  • Safety shutdown
Book a diagnosis

Fluctuating Water Temperature

Temperature swings are typically caused by sudden changes in water demand or an undersized unit. Scale buildup in the heat exchanger and faulty temperature or flow sensors also lead to inconsistent heating.

  • Sudden demand changes
  • Undersized unit
  • Heat exchanger scale
  • Faulty temperature sensors
Book a diagnosis

Cold Water Sandwich Effect

This temporary burst of cold water occurs when a tap is turned off and on quickly, reflecting normal on-demand heating rather than a breakdown. Adding a recirculation system can help eliminate this issue.

Ask about recirculation

Low Water Flow

Reduced water volume is typically caused by scale buildup, inlet filter restrictions, or clogged plumbing lines. Malfunctioning flow sensors and high simultaneous household demand can also limit overall flow.

  • Scale build-up
  • Clogged inlet filter
  • Restricted plumbing
  • Faulty flow sensor
  • High simultaneous demand
Book a diagnosis

Water Leaks

Leaks often stem from loose fittings, worn valves, pressure issues, or heat exchanger corrosion. Because water, gas, and electrical components share a compact space, leaks require prompt professional attention.

  • Loose fittings
  • Worn valves
  • Pressure problems
  • Heat exchanger corrosion
Get help now

Unit Shutting Off / Short Cycling

Premature shutdowns usually happen when safety controls detect flow irregularities, venting blockages, or inconsistent gas supply. Scale buildup and failing sensors can also trigger automatic safety cutoffs.

  • Flow irregularities
  • Venting blockage
  • Inconsistent gas supply
  • Scale build-up
  • Failing sensors

What You Can Safely Check Before Calling a Technician

A few simple observations can help our technician diagnose the problem faster. Stick to looking, never to repairing.

Power Supply

Whether the unit is receiving power.

Breaker

Whether a relevant breaker has tripped.

Gas Supply

Whether the gas supply has been unintentionally shut off.

Display

What, if anything, the unit's display is showing.

Intake & Exhaust

Whether outdoor intake or exhaust openings are visibly blocked.

Scope of Issue

Whether the issue affects every fixture in the home or just one.

What Not to Do by Yourself

Tankless systems involve gas, electricity, combustion and venting, all of which carry real safety risk if handled incorrectly. This type of work should be left to a qualified technician.

Tankless water heater Replacement vs. Repair

When Replacement Makes Sense

Consider a new unit when ongoing issues and rising costs make replacement the smarter long-term solution.

  • Frequent breakdowns
  • Costly component failure
  • Higher energy bills
  • Unit is 10+ years old
  • Better performance with a new system

When Repair Makes Sense

A repair can often restore your system quickly and affordably when the issue is minor and the unit is still in good condition.

  • Unit is otherwise operating well
